# Audit readiness (RFC 0002, Gate D)

This document is what an external auditor receives before scoping the
independent review that Gate D requires
(`spec/rfc-0002-product-completion.md`, §13).
It is a map with citations, not a copy of the design docs: it says what to
review, in what order, what we already know is weak, and where the evidence
lives. Citations marked **[branch]** point at open pull requests, not `main`.

## 1. Scope, ranked by what a defect there costs

1. **Threshold signing and one-time share release.** The FROST Ed25519
   provider (`crates/crypto-provider-frost-ed25519/src/lib.rs:228`) binds
   every signing session to a ledger record and durably consumes that record,
   bound to `SHA-256(message)`, before any share leaves memory
   (`lib.rs:27`, allocation at `lib.rs:654`, the consume-before-release step
   at `lib.rs:764`, replay refusal at `lib.rs:698`). Shares at rest are
   sealed with ChaCha20-Poly1305 (`lib.rs:32`). A defect here signs twice,
   or signs the wrong bytes, with institution funds.
2. **The one-time-state ledger's durability contract.** `consume` appends a
   checksummed canonical-CBOR frame and `fsync`s before returning
   (`crates/one-time-state-ledger/src/lib.rs:16`); a torn final frame is
   defined recoverable (`lib.rs:40`). The share-release invariant above is
   only as strong as this property.
3. **Envelope verification in the signer.** `verify_inner`
   (`crates/signer/src/verify.rs:92`) is the policy boundary every
   authorization crosses: verifier-build pinning against the roster's
   accepted builds (`verify.rs:205`, `verify.rs:208`,
   `crates/signer/src/lib.rs:171`) and the finalized-envelope path
   (`crates/signer/src/finalized.rs:69`).
4. **Membership and the admin quorum.** `AdminTrust`
   (`crates/membership/src/lib.rs:181`), threshold validation
   (`lib.rs:353`, `lib.rs:370`) and committee checks (`lib.rs:470`) decide
   who can change who holds keys.
5. **The replicated authority** (`crates/replicated-authority/src/`):
   reservation, finality and follower state feed every exposure decision.
6. **Transport.** `services/signer-net` sits on commonware pinned to
   `=2026.9.0` (`services/signer-net/Cargo.toml:20`); CI refuses ALPHA
   stability APIs in the production profile (`.github/workflows/rust.yml:26`).
7. **QuorumVault** **[branch feat/evm-quorum-vault, PR #21]**: the 2-of-3
   owner check and duplicate-signer refusal
   (`contracts/src/QuorumVault.sol:55-56`). About 80 lines; it is the only
   Solidity in scope and is labeled a development reference.
8. **Audit chain and checkpoints.** Signed events and signed
   `(sequence, head_event_hash)` checkpoints
   (`crates/audit/src/lib.rs:3`, `lib.rs:10`, `lib.rs:12`).

## 2. Known soft spots — do not spend audit time rediscovering these

* **One-machine development DKG.** `oic-node provision` runs the whole DKG
  on the machine that provisions, and says so
  (**[branch feat/node-runner]** `services/node-runner/src/provision.rs:79`,
  the failure-domain note at `provision.rs:117`). There is no key ceremony;
  each share is not created on its own machine. Development only.
* **Software sealing.** Shares are sealed in software; there is no HSM or
  TEE anywhere (`crates/crypto-provider-frost-ed25519/src/lib.rs:32`).
* **Checkpoint keys live with the operator.** THREAT_MODEL.md T6 states it
  plainly: the development stack keeps the checkpoint-signing key and the
  checkpoints in the operator's own state directory, so signed checkpoints
  do not yet bind the operator.
* **Single-provider chain trust.** The RPC clients self-declare
  `EvidenceTrustLevel::SingleProvider`
  (**[branch feat/chain-rpc-clients, PR #19]**
  `services/chain-rpc/src/evm.rs:62`): one node's answers are the evidence.
* **Threshold ECDSA is an open decision.** RFC 0002 §3 decision 1
  (`spec/rfc-0002-product-completion.md:44`) requires an audited backend and
  forbids improvised wrappers; until it is made, EVM EOAs cannot be
  threshold-signed here. The vault (scope item 7) is the interim route.

## 3. Evidence inventory

* **Acceptance coverage.** 30 of the 38 RFC 0001 acceptance scenarios are
  cited by passing tests; 5 await chain adapters that do not exist, 3 await
  recovery/attestation machinery (**[branch test/production-transport-multi-ip,
  PR #20]** `docs/acceptance-coverage.md`, regenerated by
  `scripts/acceptance_coverage.py`).
* **Real-node proofs.** On live development nodes, through the shipped
  clients: a FROST 2-of-3 signature accepted by a Solana validator and a
  full EVM loop (**[PR #19]** `services/chain-rpc/tests/`), and the
  quorum-vault deposit/withdrawal/replay-refusal flow (**[PR #21]**
  `services/chain-rpc/tests/evm_vault.rs`).
* **Topology and recovery.** The five-node reference topology runs as real
  OS processes and as one-IP-per-node containers, converging in 128 s and
  109 s respectively (**[branch feat/node-runner]**
  `services/node-runner/tests/topology.rs`, `deploy/docker/qualify.sh`).
  The single-signer restart drill exists there; its re-convergence timing
  is still being qualified and is not yet evidence.
* **Release integrity.** Tagged releases ship cosign signatures, SLSA build
  provenance and smoke installs (`.github/workflows/release.yml`); the
  custody check runs in consumer CI via the composite action (`action.yml`).

## 5. Suggested phasing

* **Phase 1 — the money-loss core.** The FROST provider with the ledger
  underneath it (scope items 1–2), against RFC 0001 §6.3/A11: can any
  interleaving of crashes, replays or malformed peer messages release a
  share twice or over different bytes than were consumed?
* **Phase 2 — authorization.** Envelope verification, membership, the
  replicated authority (items 3–5): can anything signed ever exceed what
  policy, quorum and exposure accounting permit?
* **Phase 3 — the edges.** Transport stability assumptions, the vault
  contract, release integrity (items 6–8).

Phase 1 findings invalidate everything after them, so they come first; the
contract is last because it is small, replaceable and already fenced as a
development reference.
